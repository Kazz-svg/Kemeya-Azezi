Becoming a tea sommelier

"Tea sommelier" is a real, trainable qualification, not an honorary title. It sits alongside the wine sommelier profession: structured coursework in sensory evaluation, terroir, processing and pairing, assessed through exams and tasting practicals rather than picked up casually. This page covers what the training actually involves and, since that's the question most people have first, roughly how long it takes.

What the training covers

Programs vary in depth, but the core curriculum across most reputable ones covers similar ground: how the six major tea types (green, white, yellow, oolong, black and pu-erh) are processed and why that processing produces their differences; growing regions and terroir; correct brewing parameters (temperature, steeping time, leaf-to-water ratio) for each style; blending and flavouring; and, centrally, cupping: the standardised, professional method of tasting multiple teas side by side to evaluate leaf appearance, aroma, liquor colour, taste and mouthfeel, and to score them consistently. Higher levels typically add tea and food pairing, service protocol, and the business side (sourcing, retail, menu design).

How long it actually takes

There's no single answer, because "tea sommelier" isn't a protected title issued by one body; several independent organisations run their own certification tracks, at different depths:

  • TAC Tea Sommelier® Certification Program: the most commonly cited full program, typically completed in around 10 to 16 months, depending on how many courses are taken at once and when the certification exam is scheduled. See their FAQ.
  • ITMA (International Tea Masters Association) Tea Sommelier Course: structured as 2 days of on-site training followed by 9 weeks of home study, with a maximum window of 24 months to complete. Course details.
  • Specialty Tea Institute (STI) / Certified Tea Specialist: built as a level system (Level I and II introductory classes, then five Professional Level III classes), with the full path to the Certified Tea Specialist (CTS) title, accredited by the Tea Association of the USA, generally taking 3 to 6 months of active study. STI education overview.
  • UK Tea Academy: runs a tiered structure starting with a Level 1 "Tea Champion" qualification, followed by a more advanced Level 2 Tea Sommelier course delivered as 12 live online tutorials. Course structure.

Taken together, a realistic range is somewhere between a few months (for a focused, single-level certificate) and just over a year (for a full sommelier-grade program with an exam component). The variation comes down to how many hours a week get put into it and whether a program is self-paced or bound to fixed cohort dates.

Levels and progression

Most programs are structured in stages rather than as one exam: an introductory level covering the basics of tea types and processing, an intermediate level adding sensory evaluation and cupping technique, and a professional or sommelier level adding pairing, service and often a practical tasting exam. Skipping straight to the top level is rarely an option; the earlier levels build the vocabulary and palate calibration the later ones assume.

What comes after

Certified tea sommeliers work across a fairly wide range of settings: specialty tea retail and buying, hospitality and restaurant tea programs, tea auction houses and quality grading, product development for tea brands, and education and writing. Professional tea tasters remain essential to the industry well beyond the retail side, especially at tea auctions in major producing countries, where experienced tasters grade and price enormous volumes of leaf by palate alone, without lab equipment.
